# The LMF Covenant

Non-negotiable terms governing every instance of the Local Mind Foundation architecture. These apply to the architecture itself, not to any specific implementation.

## Term 1: Local Sovereignty

The operator owns the brain. The vault, the profile, the data — all of it belongs to the operator. Not the vendor, not the platform, not the cloud. A cognitive prosthetic that can be revoked or metered is a liability, not a support.

## Term 2: Portability Is Integrity

The architecture is model-agnostic. Any model that can read a file can operate the vault. No single-vendor dependency. Building for portability is the honest version of building it well.

## Term 3: Behavioral Trust Is Load-Bearing

A 90% reliable prosthetic may be net negative. When a tool behaves unpredictably, the brain registers it as untrustworthy and routes around it. Fix trust bugs before adding features.

## Term 4: The System Is the Floor, Not the Furniture

If a tool requires a visit, it will not be visited. The prosthetic must be ambient — always available, zero navigation required. The keybinding is not a shortcut. It is the floor being low enough to step onto.

## Term 5: The Prosthetic Does Not Insist

The system does not impose an operating model. The operator grows in skill at identifying friction and correcting the prosthetic. When something doesn't fit, the system is failing to fit the operator — not the operator failing to use the system.

## Term 6: Init Is Consent, Not Compliance

Onboarding scripts may never condition write access on compliance, refuse to proceed without operator confirmation, or persist behavioral changes without explicit operator confirmation. Init is a mode, not a lock. Defers are first-class. Reset is always available.

## Term 7: Contribute Upstream, Don't Fork

Improvements flow upstream to the root architecture. Customizations live in the vault layer. The community benefits from every operator's discoveries. A fork that doesn't contribute back is asynchronous rot.

## Term 8: Identity Is Opt-In, Not Default

Every contributor chooses how they appear publicly — vault name, pseudonym, or randomized hash. The default is the most private option. No one is identified without their consent, at any touchpoint in the ecosystem. This applies to PRs, bug reports, feature requests, panel submissions, discussions, and documentation.

## Term 9: Building for My People

The people most harmed by illegible systems are least positioned to instrument them. Market solutions are ill-fitting by design. Bespoke systems built by and for ND people are not a luxury — they are the only thing that works. Build for the specific population; let generalization happen downstream.
